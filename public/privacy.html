Privacy policy

DropRadar helps independent artists promote their releases and build a fan list they own. This page explains, in plain language, what we collect and what we do with it.

If you are a fan who joined an artist's list

  • What we keep: your email address, the release page you signed up on, which link brought you there (for example "tiktok"), and the date.
  • Who sees it: only the artist whose list you joined. We never sell or rent your email, and other artists cannot see it.
  • What you agreed to: emails from that artist about their music. Nothing else.
  • Leaving: you can unsubscribe at any time with the unsubscribe link in the artist's emails. Once you do, the artist can no longer export your address from DropRadar.

If you are an artist with a DropRadar account

  • Account: your email, artist name and a securely hashed password (we never store the password itself).
  • Releases: the details you enter, such as title, genre, mood, story and streaming links.
  • Security log: sign-ins, failed attempts and lockouts, with the network address they came from, so you can spot anyone trying to get into your account.
  • Your fan list belongs to you. You can view and export it at any time. You are responsible for only emailing fans about your music and honoring unsubscribes.

Link clicks

When someone taps a platform button on a smart link, we record the platform, the tracking source and the day. We do not store the visitor's network address or identity for clicks.

Cookies

DropRadar uses one cookie: a secure sign-in cookie for artists. There are no advertising or tracking cookies.

Deleting your data

Artists can delete any release, which removes its fans and clicks. To delete an account or ask any privacy question, open a request on our GitHub page.
